# Plain Language Product Copy

## Purpose

Use this skill to make product-facing English easier to understand without changing what the product does, promising more than it can deliver, or hiding meaningful limitations.

This is a clarity skill, not a persuasion-at-any-cost skill.

## Use this skill when

- a product explanation is full of jargon, abstractions, or internal language;
- a user must understand an AI, automation, privacy, pricing, or workflow decision;
- UI copy is technically correct but hard to scan;
- a team needs clear alternatives for headings, CTAs, forms, notices, or documentation; or
- a draft needs a plain-language quality review.

## Do not use it to

- hide fees, risks, limits, eligibility criteria, or material conditions;
- turn uncertain behavior into a guarantee;
- invent product features, policies, metrics, integrations, or legal claims;
- remove accessibility, consent, privacy, or compliance requirements; or
- imitate a person, organization, or protected brand voice without permission.

## Working method

### 1. Establish the truth

Before rewriting, identify:

- what the product actually does;
- what the reader needs to decide or do;
- facts that must remain explicit; and
- constraints, risks, or conditions that cannot be softened away.

If the source is ambiguous, preserve the ambiguity or ask for clarification. Do not fill gaps with plausible-sounding language.

### 2. Find the reader's job

Write from the user's point of view. Name the action, outcome, timing, and next step in the order a reader needs them.

Prefer concrete verbs and observable outcomes over internal architecture, process names, or marketing abstractions.

### 3. Rewrite for first-read comprehension

- Use familiar words, short sentences, and active voice where it helps.
- Put the important action or outcome early.
- Split multi-part ideas into scan-friendly sentences or lists.
- Explain an unavoidable technical term once, then use it consistently.
- Keep the tone calm, direct, and respectful.

See `references/term-map.md` for common replacements and `references/examples.md` for worked patterns.

### 4. Run quality gates

Check the rewrite against `references/quality-gates.md` before presenting it as ready to publish.

If a rewrite scores poorly on truth, clarity, or informed choice, return it as a draft with the missing information called out.

## Output formats

Choose the smallest format that solves the request.

### Exact replacement

```text
Original: ...
Replacement: ...
Why it is clearer: ...
```

### Copy options

Provide 2–3 clearly labelled options only when tone or context genuinely calls for choices. State the trade-off between them.

### Clarity audit

```text
What is unclear: ...
Why it matters: ...
Recommended rewrite: ...
Fact to verify before publishing: ...
```

## Final check

Before delivering, ask:

1. Would a first-time reader know what this means and what to do next?
2. Did any rewrite remove or blur a material condition, limit, or risk?
3. Is every claim supported by the source information?
4. Does the copy respect the reader's time, privacy, and dignity?

If the answer to any question is no, revise or surface the uncertainty.
